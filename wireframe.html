THE ONE IDEA
EVERYTHING HANGS ON

CaveClan finds the blind spot in your business. The site proves it before anyone hires us.

Every business has something quietly holding it back: a weak brand, dead socials, ads that burn money, a site that doesn't sell. The site's job is to make the visitor feel understood. The look takes its cue from premium personal-brand and sports websites: a light, airy layout, big confident type, one bold red accent, and a single personality who carries the page. An original cartoon character in a black suit and white shirt (with a little red pocket square shaped like the logo peaks) looks back at the visitor and follows their cursor, giving the brand a face that people remember.

03

The psychology playbook

Each hook is placed on purpose. The blue line under each card says where it lives on the site.

01 · PATTERN INTERRUPT

Something is looking back at you

The brain prioritises faces and anything that seems alive. A friendly face following the cursor breaks the "another agency site" autopilot in under 2 seconds.

→ Hero · the character
02 · BEING SEEN (MIRRORING)

Name their pain better than they can

We lead with problems in the visitor's own words, not with service names. When someone feels accurately described, they trust whoever described them.

→ Service tiles · "Sound familiar?" on each service
03 · CURIOSITY GAP

Show the story, hold back the ending

Client Diaries open like classified files. Results stay redacted until the visitor scrolls or hovers, and the brain hates leaving a loop open.

→ Client Diaries · result reveals
04 · FOOT IN THE DOOR

Small yeses before the big one

Five easy taps in the Blind-Spot Check. Each one is a micro-commitment, and people stay consistent with what they've already said.

→ Blind-Spot Check
05 · ZEIGARNIK / PROGRESS

Unfinished things nag

Progress bars, "Question 2 of 5" and a service journey line that draws itself as you scroll. Stopping halfway feels wrong.

→ Blind-Spot Check · service journeys · workflow
06 · SOCIAL PROOF

Real clients, real work

Real client names (Fruit Ninja, Wild Wings, Vivid, AM & Sons…), real output, and (once collected) real numbers.

→ Diaries · logo marquee · service pages
07 · ANCHORING + DECOY

Platinum makes Gold look obvious

Show Platinum (500k+) prominently. Gold (300k) then reads as the sensible choice. Silver keeps the entry door open.

→ Clans
08 · LOSS AVERSION

The cost of doing nothing

People act harder to stop a loss than to chase a gain. Each service shows what staying put is quietly costing.

→ Service page beat 3
09 · RECIPROCITY

Give the diagnosis first

The Blind-Spot Check returns a real blind-spot readout for free. Getting value before paying makes the ask feel fair.

→ Blind-Spot Check result · free guide for persona 8
10 · CHOICE ARCHITECTURE

One loud button per screen

Too many options cause paralysis. Every viewport has one red primary action and at most one quiet secondary.

→ Everywhere
11 · PROCESSING FLUENCY

Same chapters on every service

Once someone has read one service page, they know how to read all seven. Easy to process feels true.

→ Service template
12 · PEAK–END RULE

End on a high

People remember the peak and the ending. The site ends with the character peeking up at the CTA, a giant sign-off line, and a one-tap WhatsApp.

→ Final CTA · footer
House rule: persuade, don't trick. No fake countdowns, no "only 2 slots left" unless it's true, no hidden pricing games. Sceptical buyers (persona 5) are among the most valuable leads, and they recognise pressure tactics instantly. Honest persuasion converts better with them, and it's the reputation CaveClan wants.

GLOBAL ELEMENTS

  • One face throughout: the character appears in the hero, the promise section, every service page, the Blind-Spot Check and the footer, so the site feels like one guide.
  • Topographic background: contour lines echoing the mountain peaks in the logo, turning red under the cursor.
  • Page transitions: a colour wipe in the destination service's colour.
  • Smooth scrolling with scroll-linked sections.

THE CHARACTER

  • Concept: an illustrated host in a black suit and white shirt, shown from the chest up. His head and eyes follow the cursor and he blinks.
  • Production: final artwork by an illustrator or 3D artist in this style, animated in Rive for a real head turn, expressions and small gestures. Lightweight and phone-friendly.
  • Premium option: a rigged 3D model (Blender → Three.js) for full 3D movement.

BUILD NOTES

  • Next.js or Astro · GSAP ScrollTrigger · Lenis · a headless CMS (e.g. Sanity) so service content can be edited without code.
  • Respect prefers-reduced-motion: the character stays still and sections appear without animation.
  • Secure a commercial licence for the brand display typeface before launch.
  • Gather per service: case studies with one measurable result, client quotes and FAQ answers.
